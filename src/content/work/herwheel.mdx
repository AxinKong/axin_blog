## Context

**HerWheel（ハーホイール）** is a two-sided marketplace that matches women in Japan who
want to drive with **vetted female driving coaches and practice partners** in their area.

Live: [herwheel-frontend.vercel.app](https://herwheel-frontend.vercel.app) · JP / EN / ZH

## 01. Problem

Japan has a large population of women who hold a driver's licence but have not driven
for years — often stopping after marriage or childbirth, then needing to drive again for
school runs and family logistics. There is also a sizeable group of foreign women living
in Japan for whom language and unfamiliar traffic norms add another layer.

Their anxieties are specific, and driving schools do not cover them:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| 路上に出るのが怖い | Afraid of driving on real roads |
| 駐車が苦手 | Can't park confidently |
| 子どもを乗せた送迎が不安 | Nervous driving with children in the car |
| 夜間運転が怖い | Afraid of driving at night |
| 高速道路が怖い | Afraid of the expressway |
| レンタカー運転が怖い | Afraid of renting a car on a trip |

These are *post-licence* problems. Driving schools teach you to pass a test; nobody
teaches you to merge onto the Shuto Expressway with two kids in the back.

## 02. The gap

There is persistent demand to be taught **by a woman** — and almost no service in Japan
lets you specify a female instructor.

That is the whole opening: not a better booking UX, not cheaper lessons. A preference that
exists, is spoken about, and has no supply attached to it.

## 03. Product

Three functions:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Coach onboarding** | Licence upload is mandatory; every coach is manually vetted before the profile goes live |
| **Search & match** | Filter by prefecture/city, transmission (AT / MT / EV), speciality (expressway, parallel parking, night driving, test prep, road-trip prep, city driving) and hourly rate |
| **Community** | Driving tips, road-trip write-ups, used-car buying experiences — the reason to come back after the lessons end |

**Why vetting is not optional.** The entire premise is that a nervous learner feels safe.
A C2C marketplace where anyone can list themselves would destroy the one thing the product
is selling. Manual review is slow and doesn't scale — and it is the correct trade at this
stage.

**Why the community exists.** Lessons end. Without a reason to return, every user is a
one-off transaction. The community is a retention bet, not a feature request.

## 04. Revenue model — and the constraint that shaped it

Learners pay through the platform. HerWheel takes **20%**; 80% goes to the coach via
Stripe Connect.

### The regulatory constraint

Holding money on behalf of a third party in Japan is a **funds-transfer business
(資金移動業)** and requires an FSA licence. For a solo founder, that is not a realistic
path.

So the payment flow was designed around the exemptions in the revised Payment Services Act:

1. **Platform-operator exemption** — the platform is an indispensable part of how the
   contract between learner and coach comes into existence, then collects and forwards
   payment under that contract.
2. **Escrow-type exemption** — funds are held only until the service is delivered, then
   released. Explicitly carved out for marketplace and sharing-economy services.

The booking flow follows from that, not from UX preference:

| Event | What happens to the money |
| --- | --- |
| Booking | Learner pays immediately; funds sit in the platform balance |
| Coach accepts | Pure acceptance — no money movement |
| Coach cancels (any time) | Full refund to learner |
| Learner cancels > 24h before | Full refund |
| Learner cancels < 24h before | Cancel button locked; dispute flow only |
| Service delivered | Confirm receipt / raise a dispute / auto-confirm after 48h |
| Dispute | Admin refunds or releases to coach |

> The 24-hour lock and the 48-hour auto-confirm are not UX decisions. They are what keeps
> the flow inside the escrow exemption while stopping a learner from consuming a coach's
> time and then walking away.

## 05. Where it actually is

The platform is deployed and works end to end: auth, coach onboarding with licence upload,
search, booking, Stripe Connect payments, cancellation and dispute handling, an admin
console, and a community board — in three languages.

**And it has zero coaches, zero bookings, and zero posts.**

## 06. What the plan says to do next

The business plan is explicit that the biggest risk **is not competition** — it is whether
women will actually pay extra to be taught by a woman.

**Phase 1 — Tokyo only, 3 coaches, 3 months**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Landing page | Coach profiles and one booking path. Nothing else. |
| Supply | Recruit and vet 3 female coaches in Tokyo |
| Channels | Test Xiaohongshu, Instagram and SEO; find which one works |
| Milestone | **20 customers who actually pay**, within 3 months |

If those 20 convert and some return or refer, the demand hypothesis holds and the full
platform goes live. If not, it doesn't.

**Unit economics assumed in the plan**: ¥4,000/hour average lesson, ~70 lessons/month at
MVP scale → ¥280,000 GMV → ¥56,000 revenue at a 20% take rate. Loss-making by design
during validation. 【TBD: none of this is tested.】

## 07. The honest part

**I built the platform before validating the demand.**

The riskiest assumption — that this preference converts into payment — is the one thing
the code cannot answer. A landing page with three coaches and a booking form would have
tested it in a few weeks. Instead there is a full marketplace with escrow, dispute
resolution and three-language support, serving nobody.

The build was not wasted: it exists, it works, and it is ready the moment demand shows up.
But the order was wrong, and the plan's own Phase 1 is effectively a correction — go back
and do the cheap test that should have come first.
